## 1. Phase 9 Objective & Architectural Mandate

The primary objective of Phase 9 is to translate the comprehensive, validated empirical evidence from Phases 1–8 into an interactive, public-facing analytical diagnostic tool.

### Crucial Architectural Rules:
1. **Presentation & Interaction Layer Only:** The dashboard is strictly a presentation, exploration, and storytelling layer over the validated analytical infrastructure. It must **not** become a secondary analytical engine.
2. **Re-use of Authoritative Analytical Engines:** All underlying data queries, aggregations, group comparisons, period trends, and narrative generation must call directly into:
   - `scripts/dataset_1_analytics.py` (D1 filtering, multi-dimensional analysis, trends, period changes)
   - `scripts/dataset_1_insights.py` (structured insight extraction)
   - `scripts/dataset_1_narratives.py` (deterministic, non-causal natural language narrative synthesis)
   - `scripts/dataset_2_analytics.py` (D2 non-agricultural enterprise composition, structural absence handling)
3. **No Redundant Data Processing:** The UI must not perform ad-hoc aggregations or create disconnected CSVs. Every visualization and KPI card maps directly from structured engine outputs.
4. **Strict Evidence Integrity:** All descriptive, statistical (Phase 6), predictive (Phase 7), and diagnostic (Phase 8) boundaries established in previous phases are fully maintained. No casual or unsupported claims are introduced.

---

## 2. Core UI/UX Design Principles

The application is engineered as an **analytical diagnostic tool**, distinct from a standard corporate business intelligence dashboard:
- **Progressive Disclosure:** Users move from high-level national patterns to state-specific comparisons, deep structural cross-sections (education, rural/urban, enterprise), and formal inferential/predictive validation.
- **Analytical Clarity over Decoration:** Every visual element serves a diagnostic function. Charts display meaningful comparisons (female vs. male, rural vs. urban, 2017 vs. 2023) rather than raw isolated numbers.
- **Context Near Visuals:** Every visual output is paired with a deterministic narrative block generated by `dataset_1_narratives.py` or documented Phase 6/8 evidence.
- **Methodological Transparency:** Warnings regarding unweighted analytical means, compositional data constraints, and structural absences are displayed inline without obstructing exploration.
- **Clear Missingness Semantics:** The UI explicitly differentiates between `Value is Zero` (0.0%), `Data Structurally Absent` (e.g., Division `014, 016, 017, 02-99` in 2022–2023), and `Comparison Unavailable` (e.g., missing matched pair).

## 4. Detailed Section Specifications

### Section 1: National Diagnostic Overview
- **Purpose:** Provide an authoritative landing experience establishing the macro trajectory of women's labour participation in India between 2017 and 2023.
- **User Question:** *"What has happened to women's labour-force participation across India between 2017 and 2023, and how persistent is the gender gap?"*
- **Primary Dimensions:** Gender (`Female`, `Male`, `Persons`), Year (`2017–2023`), Area_Type (`Rural + Urban`).
- **Primary Indicators:** LFPR, WPR, Gender Participation Gap ($\text{LFPR}_{\text{Female}} - \text{LFPR}_{\text{Male}}$).
- **Core Visualisations:**
  1. *Headline Metric Ribbon (3 Cards):* 2023 National Female LFPR (45.0%), 2017–2023 Change (+19.2 pp), 2023 Female-Male Gap ($-33.1$ pp).
  2. *Dual-Line Temporal Trajectory Chart:* Female LFPR vs. Male LFPR from 2017 to 2023, highlighting steady male participation (~75–78%) alongside rising female participation.
  3. *Five Diagnostic Theme Accordion:* Interactive summary cards detailing the five core Phase 8 diagnostic findings.
- **Backend Re-use:** `dataset_1_analytics.trend()`, `dataset_1_analytics.change()`, `dataset_1_narratives.generate_narratives()`.

---

### Section 2: State Diagnostic Explorer & Context Profiles
- **Purpose:** Provide a unified diagnostic dossier for any selected Indian State/UT, contextualising its performance within the 4 descriptive labour-market archetypes established in Phase 8.
- **User Question:** *"What makes this State's labour market unique, where does it sit relative to the national average, and which archetype does it represent?"*
- **Primary Dimensions:** State (dropdown of 36 States/UTs), Year (slider or default 2023), Indicator (`LFPR`, `WPR`, `Unemployment_Rate`).
- **Core Visualisations & Outputs:**
  1. *Archetype Badge & Contextual Summary:* Displays which of the 4 descriptive archetypes the State aligns with:
     - *High-Participation Agrarian & Hill Context*
     - *Rapid-Catchup Northern & Eastern Agrarian Context*
     - *Moderate-Participation Peninsular & Diversified Context*
     - *Low-Participation Urban & Northern Enclave Context*
  2. *State KPI Ribbon:* State Female LFPR, State Gender Gap, State Rural-Urban Gap, 2017–2023 Net Change.
  3. *Dumbbell Comparison Chart (Gender Gap):* Female vs. Male participation for the selected State vs. National benchmark.
  4. *Multi-Year State Trajectory:* 2017–2023 path with optional comparison state overlay.
  5. *Dynamic Narrative Card:* Auto-generated natural language summary from `dataset_1_narratives.py`.
- **Backend Re-use:** `dataset_1_analytics.filter_data()`, `dataset_1_analytics.compare()`, `dataset_1_narratives.generate_narratives()`.

---

### Section 3: Structural Fault Lines (Education & Rural-Urban Divergence)
- **Purpose:** Deep-dive into the two most pronounced structural non-linearities in the dataset: the educational U-curve and the spatial rural-urban divide.
- **User Question:** *"Why does education not linearly increase women's participation, and why did rural and urban trajectories diverge?"*
- **Primary Dimensions:** Education (8 detailed levels), Area_Type (`Rural`, `Urban`), Year, Gender.
- **Core Visualisations:**
  1. *The Educational U-Curve (Grouped Bar / Line):* Female LFPR across 8 education categories, contrasting with Male LFPR (demonstrating the trough at Middle/Secondary schooling and recovery at Diploma/Postgraduate).
  2. *Educated Unemployment Friction Plot (Dumbbell/Bar):* Female vs. Male Unemployment Rate across education tiers, showcasing the extreme gender gap at Graduate (23.3% vs. 10.7%) and Postgraduate levels.
  3. *Rural-Urban Widening Slope/Area Chart:* Tracking the expansion of the rural-urban female gap from $4.7$ pp in 2017 to $19.8$ pp in 2023.
- **Backend Re-use:** `dataset_1_analytics.analyse(by=['Education'])`, Phase 6 statistical findings (F4, F5, F6, F8).

---

### Section 4: Employment & Enterprise Structure
- **Purpose:** Examine the institutional and enterprise landscape of non-agricultural work from Dataset 2.
- **User Question:** *"In what kinds of enterprises do working women find employment, and how does this differ from men?"*
- **Primary Dimensions:** Enterprise_Type (8 categories), Industry_Division_Type, Gender, Area_Type, Year.
- **Primary Indicators:** Percentage Engaged (% share).
- **Core Visualisations:**
  1. *Horizontal Stacked Bar / Diverging Bar:* Female vs. Male employment distribution across enterprise types in Industry Division `(05-99)`.
  2. *The Public Sector Anchor Callout:* Visual comparison highlighting female over-representation in Government/Local Body enterprises (24.9% vs. 16.0% male) and Employer Households (6.3% vs. 0.8% male), alongside male concentration in Proprietary units (68.0% vs. 56.6% female).
  3. *Structural Missingness & Aggregation Warning Box:* Explicit notice detailing that Division `(014, 016, 017, 02-99)` is absent in 2022–2023, and reminding users that enterprise percentages are compositional.
- **Backend Re-use:** `dataset_2_analytics.compare(dimension='Enterprise_Type')`, `dataset_2_analytics.STRUCTURAL_ABSENCE_LABEL`.

---

### Section 5: Statistical Evidence, Predictive Limits & Methodology
- **Purpose:** Establish complete scientific traceability by exposing the Phase 6 hypothesis tests and Phase 7 predictive modeling results.
- **User Question:** *"Which observed patterns are statistically verified, what can demographic factors predict, and what remains unexplained?"*
- **Core Elements:**
  1. *Phase 6 Inferential Validation Table:* Interactive filterable table displaying findings F1 through F11, including test type (Wilcoxon, Friedman, Permutation), test statistic, sample size ($n=251$ for paired F2), $p$-value, effect size ($r$ / $\epsilon^2$), and plain-language interpretation.
  2. *Phase 7 Machine Learning Explanatory Limits:*
     - Model comparison table (Baseline $R^2 = -0.008$, Ridge $R^2 = 0.139$, GBR $R^2 = 0.155$ under 36-fold LOSO).
     - Visual breakdown of the **Predictive Ceiling**: Demographic factors (Education, Area Type, Year) explain ~15.5% of out-of-fold variance, leaving ~84.5% unexplained by these demographic predictors, highlighting the critical role of unobserved state-specific context.
     - Feature attribution chart (Standardized Year, Middle/Primary Education, and Urbanization).
  3. *Methodological Caveat Library:* Clear documentation of unweighted means, lack of wage data, and non-causal boundaries.
- **Backend Re-use:** `outputs/phase_6_statistical_analysis/statistical_results.csv`, `outputs/phase_7_ml/model_results.csv`.

## 5. Analytical User Journeys

### User Journey A: The Policy Maker / General Explorer (Macro Overview)
1. **Entry (Tab 1):** Lands on the National Overview. Immediately sees headline metric cards showing the 2017–2023 surge (+19.2 pp) alongside the persistent gap ($-33.1$ pp).
2. **First Interaction:** Toggles between Female, Male, and Both on the temporal trajectory line chart to observe male stability vs. female expansion.
3. **Exploration (Tab 3):** Navigates to Structural Fault Lines to inspect why the national trend occurred. Observes that rural women and lower-educated women drove the increase, while urban women and educated women face stagnation and high unemployment.
4. **Contextualisation (Tab 4):** Checks the Enterprise Structure tab to see where women work (public sector anchor and domestic service).
5. **Conclusion (Tab 5):** Reviews the Statistical Evidence tab to confirm that these findings are formally backed by inferential tests ($p < 0.001$).

### User Journey B: The State Diagnostic Analyst / Researcher (Regional Deep-Dive)
1. **Entry & State Selection (Tab 2):** Navigates directly to the State Diagnostic Explorer. Selects a specific state (e.g., *Bihar* or *Tamil Nadu*).
2. **Diagnostic Readout:**
   - Observes the State Archetype badge (e.g., *Bihar* classified under *Rapid-Catchup Northern & Eastern Agrarian Context*; *Tamil Nadu* under *Moderate-Participation Peninsular & Diversified Context*).
   - Reads the auto-generated deterministic narrative explaining the State's 2017–2023 trajectory.
3. **Comparison:** Adds a second comparator State (e.g., *Kerala* vs. *Bihar*) to examine divergent rural-urban divides and graduate unemployment rates.
4. **Methodological Verification (Tab 5):** Navigates to Tab 5 to examine why the predictive model had high or negative out-of-fold $R^2$ for the selected state, recognizing the importance of local context over national demographic averages.

## 6. Interaction Model & Filter Hierarchy

To avoid confusing filter matrices, the dashboard employs a **tiered filter architecture**:

1. **State Selector:** Primary dropdown on Tab 2 with search capabilities across all 36 States/UTs.
2. **Temporal Controls:** Year selector slider (2017 to 2023, default 2023) available in Tab 2 and Tab 3.
3. **Indicator Toggles:** Radio buttons for `LFPR`, `WPR`, `Unemployment_Rate` (default `LFPR`).
4. **Subpopulation Filters:** Education and Area_Type selectors in Tab 3 with "All" preselected to maintain comparability.
5. **Reset & Safe Defaults:** Every tab includes a "Reset to National Baseline" action.

### Handling Missingness & Structural Absences:
- If a user selects Chandigarh, 2023, Rural: The UI renders: `[Notice: Data Structurally Absent in Source for Rural Chandigarh 2023]`. It does **not** plot zero.
- In Dataset 2 (Tab 4): If Year 2022 or 2023 is viewed, Industry Division `(014, 016, 017, 02-99)` displays: `[Notice: Category Structurally Absent in 2022–2023 Source Survey]`.
